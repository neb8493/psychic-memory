## 1. What the channel is

- **Premise:** an animated world "made just for dogs," starring two recurring pups, **Pluto and Blue**.
- **Origin story (their about text):** their own dog Pluto struggled with stress; relaxing,
  dog-safe videos calmed her; now Pluto and Blue "bring that same calm energy to homes around the world."
- **Promise to the viewer (the owner):** soothing music, peaceful nature scenes, colorful
  "tail-wagging" cartoons, and endless adventures with Pluto & Blue.
- **Nearest competitors using the same formula:** Cartoons for Dogs TV (Bluey, Luna & Friends),
  Max & Milo TV, ScoutPaw, Relax My Dog, DogTV.

## 2. Who the videos are really for (two audiences)

| Audience | What they need | Where the channel serves it |
|---|---|---|
| The **owner** (the one who clicks and presses play) | Guilt relief before leaving the house; a reason to believe it works | Title, thumbnail, description, the origin story, "colors they can see" science claim |
| The **dog** (the one who watches) | Slow motion, high-contrast blue/yellow, familiar dog shapes, calm low-frequency audio | The animation and music itself |

Every title on the channel is written for the owner. Every frame is designed for the dog.
Your script has to do both.

## 4. Their recurring scenarios

From titles and descriptions, the episodes are simple "day out" journeys:

1. **Town walk** (Pluto & Blue's Walk to Explore the Town): leave the house, stroll past shops,
   meet other dogs, return home.
2. **Travel episode** (Trip to Italy): same walk structure, new postcard backdrop.
3. **Nature loop:** peaceful nature scenes (meadow, lake, forest) with minimal action.
4. **Puppy edition:** same content, softer pace, marketed at new-puppy owners.
5. **Marathon compilations:** 8/12/24-hour re-cuts of the same chapters for "all day" use.

Structure of a chapter is always: **arrive, explore, small gentle surprise, rest, move on.**
Nothing is at stake. No villain, no jump, no fast cut.

## 5. Why it works for the dog (design rules to copy)

- **Palette:** dogs see blue and yellow. Backgrounds and characters use saturated blue/yellow
  and neutral greys/whites. Reds and greens read as muddy brown to a dog, so keep them out
  of anything that should catch attention.
- **Motion:** slow, continuous movement across the screen (a dog trotting left to right, a
  ball rolling, birds drifting). No strobing, no quick cuts, a cut every 8 to 20 seconds at most.
- **Familiar shapes:** dogs respond most to other dogs. Your own dogs' silhouettes on screen
  are the core asset.
- **Audio:** soft music at roughly 60 to 100 BPM, rich in lower frequencies, no sudden peaks.
  Light ambient sound (birds, water, distant park). Use dog vocalizations sparingly; a
  single friendly bark or squeak every few minutes is a re-engagement cue, constant barking is a stressor.
- **Length:** long enough to cover a work day. Build a 30-minute episode from loopable
  5-minute chapters, then publish 1-hour, 4-hour and 12-hour versions of the same file.

## 6. Why it works for the owner (positioning to copy)

- A **named cast with a backstory**. Pluto was the anxious dog; that one sentence is the
  channel's whole emotional pitch. Your dogs are real; that is a stronger version of this.
- **Reassurance language** in every title: home alone, anxiety, boredom, calming.
- **Proof cues:** "colors they can see," "dog TV," "music for dogs." Owners want to
  feel they picked something purpose-built, not a kids' cartoon.
- **Zero-effort usage:** press play, leave. Long runtime and chapters are the feature.

## 7. Script: "[DOG A] & [DOG B]'s Big Day Out"

Replace `[DOG A]` and `[DOG B]` with your dogs' names. Character notes assume one bolder
dog (A) and one more cautious dog (B); swap if that is backwards for your two.

**Format:** 30:00 episode, six 5-minute chapters, designed to loop. Widescreen 16:9.
**Palette:** sky blue #4FA3E3, deep blue #1F4E9A, sunflower yellow #FFD23F, cream #FFF6D6,
soft grey #B8BDC7, white. No red, no green.
**Music:** one continuous instrumental bed, 72 BPM, piano and low strings, gentle swells at
chapter changes only. Ambient layer changes per chapter.
**Voice:** none on screen. Optional 12-second owner voiceover in the cold open only (see below).
**On-screen text:** none inside chapters. Chapter cards only.

### COLD OPEN (0:00 to 0:20) - the dual hook

The first 20 seconds have to grab the owner's thumb and the dog's eyes at the same time.

```
0:00  Black to cream fade. Wide shot of a sunlit living room in blue/yellow.
      [DOG A] is asleep on a yellow sofa. [DOG B] is curled in a blue bed on the floor.
      Ambient: soft clock tick, distant birds. Music bed begins, very low.

0:03  A yellow tennis ball rolls slowly in from screen-left and stops against [DOG B]'s bed.
      (Slow, horizontal motion: this is the dog-attention cue.)

0:06  [DOG B]'s ear lifts. One eye opens. Ball gets a tiny nudge with the nose.

0:09  [DOG A] pops up on the sofa, tail wagging at half speed. A single soft "boof."
      (One friendly vocal, not more.)

0:12  Title card slides in from the right, yellow text on blue:
      "[DOG A] & [DOG B]'s Big Day Out"
      Sub-line, smaller, cream: "Dog TV in colors your dog can see"

0:16  OPTIONAL owner voiceover, warm and quiet:
      "This is [DOG A] and [DOG B]. [DOG B] used to hate being home alone.
       So we made them a cartoon. Press play, and go."

0:20  Title card fades. Cut to Chapter 1 card.
```

### CHAPTER 1 (0:20 to 5:00) - Good Morning at Home
Card: "1. Good Morning" (yellow on blue, 3 seconds)

```
- [DOG A] stretches on the sofa: long, slow, exaggerated. Yawn.
- [DOG B] walks the ball to [DOG A]. They touch noses. Tails wag in sync.
- Kitchen: two bowls, one blue, one yellow, fill with kibble (kibble as bright yellow dots
  falling slowly, a satisfying gentle "tick tick" sound).
- Both eat. Hold this shot a full 40 seconds. Ambient: crunching, softened.
- A butterfly (yellow) drifts through an open window and floats across the room, left to right.
  Both dogs' heads track it in unison. It exits right.
- [DOG A] goes to the front door and sits. Looks back at [DOG B]. [DOG B] trots over.
- Door opens by itself (no human on screen, ever). Warm light floods in.
- Beat of stillness: both dogs framed in the doorway, looking out. Music swells lightly.
```

### CHAPTER 2 (5:00 to 10:00) - The Walk to the Park
Card: "2. The Walk"

```
- Side-scrolling neighbourhood. Houses in cream and blue, yellow fences, a pale blue sky.
  Camera and dogs move together, left to right, at an unhurried trot.
  This is the signature shot of the whole channel style: slow lateral movement, nothing else.
- Every 30 to 45 seconds, one small event:
    * A cat in a window (grey) watches them pass. No reaction, no chase.
    * A yellow leaf falls and lands on [DOG B]'s head. [DOG B] shakes it off. [DOG A] sniffs it.
    * A mail truck (blue) rolls past in the background, silent apart from a soft hum.
    * Another dog (a friendly grey pup) trots the opposite direction; three tails wag; it passes.
- Ambient: soft footsteps on pavement, birds, a distant bicycle bell once.
- Park gates appear on the right. Both dogs pause, then trot through.
```

### CHAPTER 3 (10:00 to 15:00) - At the Park
Card: "3. The Park"

```
- Wide meadow rendered in yellow grass and blue sky (skip green on purpose).
- [DOG A] finds the yellow ball again (it has been waiting on the path).
  Nudges it; it rolls slowly downhill. [DOG A] follows at a walk, not a sprint.
- [DOG B] lies down by a pond and watches ducks (yellow) drift left to right.
  Ripples animate slowly. Hold 45 seconds. Ambient: water lapping, ducks quietly.
- [DOG A] returns with the ball, drops it next to [DOG B]. Both lie in the sun.
- Clouds pass overhead. Their shadows slide across the grass. 60-second hold.
- A single re-engagement cue at 14:00: a squeak from the ball as [DOG B] rests a paw on it.
  Both dogs' ears perk. Then settle.
```

### CHAPTER 4 (15:00 to 20:00) - Snack Time
Card: "4. Snack Time"

```
- A little blue food cart under a yellow umbrella at the edge of the park.
- A row of treats on the counter, seen at dog eye-level. Each is a simple yellow or cream shape.
- [DOG A] sits. [DOG B] sits. Two treats slide off the counter and land in front of them
  (no human hand shown, treats simply arrive).
- Slow, happy chewing. Crumbs as yellow specks. Ambient: gentle crunch, umbrella fabric in breeze.
- A picnic blanket (blue and cream check) where both dogs nap for a while.
  Camera drifts very slowly upward to a sky full of slow clouds, then back down.
  This is the "rest" beat of the loop. 90 seconds, almost nothing happens.
```

### CHAPTER 5 (20:00 to 25:00) - The Way Home
Card: "5. Heading Home"

```
- Same side-scroll as Chapter 2, reversed direction (right to left) so the dog on the sofa
  sees the walk "undo" itself. Late-afternoon light: warmer cream, longer shadows.
- The grey pup from earlier is now asleep on its porch. [DOG A] and [DOG B] look, keep walking.
- Street lamps (yellow glow) blink on one by one as they pass.
- They arrive at their own front door. It opens for them.
```

### CHAPTER 6 (25:00 to 30:00) - Bedtime
Card: "6. Bedtime"

```
- Living room again, now dusk. Lamp light in yellow.
- [DOG A] climbs onto the sofa. [DOG B] into the blue bed. The ball rolls itself to the
  foot of the bed and stops.
- Both dogs' breathing animates slowly: rise and fall, rise and fall.
- Music thins to piano only. Ambient: crickets, very soft.
- Camera pulls back slowly through the window until the house is small, the sky is deep
  blue, and a single star comes out.
- Fade to the exact first frame of the Cold Open so the loop is seamless.
```

### END CARD (last 8 seconds, only on the non-loop upload)
Yellow on blue: "[DOG A] & [DOG B] will be back tomorrow. Subscribe so your dog never
watches alone." Small line: "Made for dogs. Loved by their people."
